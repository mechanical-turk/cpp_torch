- At 12 hours and 58 minutes, Short 5 reached 104,182 plays, 62,114 accounts,

- The Reel generated 600 likes, 85 comments, 275 saves, and 134 shares.

## 2026-09-02: Short 5 Instagram retention and edit review

- The learner-supplied curve showed 43% retention at 0:19. The steepest loss

## 2026-09-02: Short 5 engagement and follower funnel

- A later Instagram Insights capture attributed 155 profile visits, 74 follows,
  and 11 bio-link clicks to actions after viewing the Reel. It also showed 627
  likes, 86 comments, six reposts, 134 shares, and 286 saves.
- Relative to 104,182 plays, the Reel produced about a 0.15% profile-visit rate
  and a 0.07% follow rate. Relative to 62,114 reached accounts, those rates were
  about 0.25% and 0.12%.
- Follows equaled 47.7% of profile visits, although Instagram reports these as
  separate actions and they must not be treated as a proven sequential funnel.
  The supported bottleneck is generating profile intent, not repairing an
  obviously weak profile conversion.
- The final technical question likely contributed to the unusually active
  discussion, but the available data cannot assign it causal ranking weight.
  Future outros must preserve a genuinely answerable question while making the
  next concrete experiment visible. Follower conversion should not be pursued
  by weakening engagement or distribution.
- The retention curve still identifies the first three seconds as the clearest
  creative weakness. The next controlled script should tighten the complete
  hook and test a question-plus-continuation outro while preserving the strong
  middle structure.
- Short 5 also used a compact `Inefficient loop?` callout and selective caption
  color emphasis, including yellow setup words and a teal payoff word. These
  treatments plausibly helped viewers parse the premise quickly after choosing
  to stay, but the current data cannot isolate either treatment as causal.
- Preserve the combined opening system in the next controlled Short: a strong
  visual, immediate delivery, readable captions, selective keyword emphasis,
  and one curiosity-driving text callout, all expressing the same promise.

## 2026-09-04: X reply distribution outlier

- A reply to `@chesscom` reached 5,164 public impressions within its first few
  hours. It received 28 measured engagements, 10 link clicks, seven profile
  visits, and two likes.
- The reply produced about 39 times the 132-impression median of the 16 original
  posts in the current dataset. It also exceeded the next-highest reply, at 305
  impressions, by about 17 times and the best original post, at 1,103
  impressions, by about 4.7 times.
- This is strong evidence that a relevant host conversation can supply far more
  reach than the account currently earns alone. It does not prove that replies
  will repeat this result. The low like rate also shows that borrowed reach is
  not automatically qualified attention.
- Begin a controlled reply-distribution test. Contribute substantive, relevant
  ideas to suitable conversations and compare impressions, engagements, profile
  visits, and link clicks with original posts. Avoid generic replies and forced
  promotion.

## 2026-09-04: Short 5 disagreement and intentional omissions

  the compiler, floating-point, and CPU execution discussions. This was a
  qualitative sample, not a complete or randomly selected comment export.
- The compiler-and-array-boundary objection had 286 likes and ten replies;

  choice: present a correct but controversial claim, then leave selected nuance

  The disagreement can begin with the premise and continue throughout the
  Short. Comments support that participation mechanism, but do not isolate its
  causal contribution to Instagram distribution.
