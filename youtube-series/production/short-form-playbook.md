# Short-Form Playbook and Checklist

Read this before planning, writing, rewriting, recording, reviewing, or
publishing a Short, Reel, TikTok, or other native vertical lesson. This is the
authoritative short-script checklist. Long-form has its own playbook.

## Required script-writing workflow

Every new or rewritten short-form script must go through these steps in order,
without a separate request to run critique. Save the checklist plan, audit,
critique, score and editorial rationale in `script-review.md`. Keep `script.md`
limited to the descriptive MP4 filename, title, narration and recording/editing
directions. Keep keyword research in `keyword-research.md`. Perform the whole
workflow without placing its supporting review inside the recording script.

1. **Checklist plan:** before the script, explain how this specific draft will
   attempt every item S01-S20. Use one concise row per ID: intended wording,
   visual, evidence, or production action, plus any known risk. “Will comply”
   is not an explanation. Do not claim the plan already passed its checks.
2. **Script:** give one canonical draft. Mark only read-aloud words with
   `>`; keep timecodes and scene directions outside narration.
3. **Checklist audit:** run the same S01-S20 against that exact draft. For each,
   give status, a specific line/beat/visual or evidence reference, points, and
   a concrete fix for any shortfall. Do not copy the plan and call it an audit.
4. **Critique:** explain the most important remaining weaknesses and their
   effect on a cold viewer. Prioritize the hook, interesting disagreement,
   comprehension, and payoff over cosmetic edits. Name the next repair;
   do not invent faults or pad the review to meet a count.
5. **Final score:** show the calculated score out of 100 and the decision
   `advance`, `rework`, or `hold`, using the rubric below.

Keep both checklist tables concise, but do not silently omit an item.
When the creator requests only a fragment, respect that scope: assess the
fragment, explain dependencies on the rest, and mark unavailable checks
UNVERIFIED. Do not invent the rest of the script to claim full compliance.
If the draft changes after scoring, rerun the affected checks and recalculate
the score. The score must always describe the draft actually handed over.

## Mandatory narrative order

Every Short must use this sequence:

1. **Hook:** sell the idea immediately without revealing the solution.
2. **Escalate with the result:** state the measured speedup, slowdown, or other
   striking result before showing what caused it. This result raises the stakes
   and opens the curiosity gap. Never invent a number; use a clear placeholder
   until production supplies the measurement.
3. **Explain the problem:** show just enough of the original code, layout, or
   behavior for a cold viewer to understand what may be going wrong.
4. **Ask why the problem occurs:** invite the viewer to diagnose the problem
   before the solution appears. Use natural, topic-specific wording rather than
   mechanically repeating “Why is this a problem?”
5. **Reveal the solution:** show the change clearly and connect it to the
   result promised near the opening.
6. **Ask why the solution works:** immediately invite the viewer to explain the
   mechanism. Keep this distinct from the earlier problem-diagnosis question.
7. **Name the next test and cut:** close with “Next, we're testing [specific
   experiment],” or an equally direct version, then cut immediately.

Do not reveal or name the solution before the measured result. The first
question opens the diagnosis; the second opens discussion of the solution.
Both questions must give the audience something specific to reason about.

## Script checklist

Each item is a question to answer, not a box to tick from intention alone.
Weights are editorial priorities, not measured contributions to reach.

### Premise and opening

- [ ] **S01 | Strong position and meaningful disagreement | 15 points.**
  Does the claim provoke surprise, disbelief, disagreement, or an urge to
  defend a familiar engineering practice? Name the challenged belief, our
  position, intended reaction, and a specific informed objection. The idea
  itself must interest a cold viewer. A neutral tutorial with a louder
  adjective does not pass. This is the number-one creative priority; every
  critique must preserve or strengthen it.

- [ ] **S02 | Highest-signal words first | 15 points.**
  Do the first words sell the subject, stakes, contradiction, or question
  within roughly the first second? Every word matters. Identify which words
  carry the signal and when they arrive. Do not bury “slower” after abstract
  setup such as “Keeping related data together made this...” A short sentence
  can still have a slow opening. Protect the first three seconds from setup,
  qualification, greetings, series context, and pauses.

- [ ] **S03 | One complete first-frame hook | 10 points.**
  Do code/image, spoken hook, readable opening caption, compact curiosity
  callout, and red arrow/circle reinforce the same promise from frame zero?
  Specify the actual elements and their placement. No blank opening,
  essential fade-in, delayed sticker, or competing messages. A callout such
  as `Inefficient loop?` should add immediate clarity, not more reading.
  Plan the phone-size, muted first-frame check.

### Demonstration, proof, and ending

- [ ] **S04 | One focused idea | 4 points.**
  Does every beat serve the same strong claim? Count concepts as well as
  words. Use a familiar example and a small visible change with meaningful
  stakes. Remove prerequisite lectures, secondary lessons, unnecessary tests,
  and ideas without immediate payoff.

- [ ] **S05 | Immediate progress and clear causality | 4 points.**
  Does the sentence after the hook begin delivering rather than repeat or
  reintroduce it? Escalate with the measured result before naming or showing
  the solution, then move directly into the problem. Show the relevant action
  as it is named. Connect result, problem, change, and consequence in one
  uninterrupted sequence. Remove repeated definitions, duplicate walkthroughs,
  ornamental transitions, and empty waiting for the reveal.

- [ ] **S06 | Correct claim and deliberate omissions | 10 points.**
  Is the explanation technically sound, and does the wording stay within the
  supplied facts or explicitly marked result placeholders? Name what we
  deliberately leave for discussion and understand that
  nuance before recording. Keep decisive conditions visible or concisely
  stated without preemptively answering every objection. No fabricated
  results, universal speedup from one test, deliberate coding mistakes,
  feigned ignorance, or contempt. An operation count is not a speedup; a
  timing difference alone does not isolate its cause. The script writer must
  not invent results, but is not responsible for measuring or independently
  verifying benchmark timings. Clearly marked placeholders do not lose points.

- [ ] **S07 | Clear comparison and evidence handoff | 5 points.**
  Does the script clearly describe what stays the same, what changes, and what
  the result will compare? Keep known decisive conditions visible or in the
  production handoff. Use supplied results or named placeholders; identify the
  measurements needed by the benchmark/production owner. Running benchmarks,
  checking assembly, and verifying measured timings are not script-writing
  checklist items. Do not deduct points or hold a script because those tasks
  are pending. Still reject a misleading comparison or fabricated result.

- [ ] **S08 | Complete, honest payoff | 5 points.**
  Does the Short state the measured result before it reveals the solution,
  then resolve its original promise with visible evidence before moving to the
  next experiment? The problem and its diagnosis question must come between
  the early result and the solution. Do not pretend a winner remains hidden
  once both timings appear, and do not leave the promised answer to a sequel.
  Assess the reveal's completeness and wording, not whether the benchmark owner
  has populated its timing placeholders. Verification belongs to production.

- [ ] **S09 | Two distinct audience questions | 5 points.**
  Before the solution appears, does the script directly ask why the problem
  occurs? Immediately after the solution appears, does it ask why that solution
  works? Use short, natural wording tied to the code or mechanism on screen.
  The questions must invite two different kinds of reasoning: diagnosis first,
  explanation second. Do not insert a recap, caveat, transition, or follow
  request between the solution and the second question. A question about the
  next experiment alone does not satisfy this check.

- [ ] **S10 | Specific continuation and immediate cut | 5 points.**
  After the solution question, does the script say “Next, we're testing
  [specific experiment],” or an equally direct version, then cut immediately?
  The next test must be real, adjacent, and concrete. Do not invent a measured
  answer or scheduled release. Preserve discussion while creating a reason to
  return. No generic requests to follow, like, comment, or visit a profile; no
  end card, dead tail, or manufactured cliffhanger.

### Visual delivery and pacing

- [ ] **S11 | Captions emphasize meaning | 3 points.**
  Are captions readable, synchronized, concise, and selectively emphasized?
  Use yellow/teal contrast for important words, with a legible outline.
  Do not color every word, duplicate the held opening caption, or obscure
  code, results, the face, or gestures. Speech, captions, callout, and visual
  must reinforce one message.

- [ ] **S12 | Recurring, purposeful annotations | 3 points.**
  Are stickers, arrows, circles, or highlights assigned to specific targets
  throughout the explanation, change, and payoff, not only the opening?
  Name their beats. Preserve legibility and the diagram's meaning; do not
  add movement or decoration unrelated to the point.

- [ ] **S13 | Presenter movement throughout | 2 points.**
  Do purposeful hands, gestures, and other interesting presenter movement
  support each stage? Name actions rather than “be engaging.” Annotations
  cannot substitute for an otherwise static presenter. Keep movements
  aligned with the idea and out of the essential text.

- [ ] **S14 | Dense delivery with enough substance | 4 points.**
  Does the complete draft teach something meaningful, rather than compressing
  the lesson into a teaser? Target 25-30 seconds with a natural, energetic
  delivery, including the why-question and continuation. There is no arbitrary
  word maximum. Use the time needed for a clear causal explanation and a useful
  payoff; cut repetition and dead time, not the substance that earns attention.
  Do not pad a complete shorter lesson just to reach the target. Use a timed
  read; word count alone cannot establish pacing. Measure final duration with
  `ffprobe` and review the complete edit. This replaces the former strict
  under-20-second rule. A draft outside the target needs a pacing review, not
  an automatic failure based only on its duration.

- [ ] **S15 | Phone-size code and coherent visuals | 3 points.**
  Can each shot fit horizontally and vertically without requiring the viewer
  to decode a dense screen? Prefer 14 visible code lines, never more than 17.
  Keep off-screen setup out of camera snippets; remove unnecessary includes,
  wrappers, templates, namespaces, and qualifiers when correctness permits.
  Use targeted `using` declarations, never `using namespace std;`, and
  short safe types such as `int`. Prefer readable one-line signatures.
  Preserve values, identities, shape grammar, and label alignment through
  visual changes. Follow the current visual guidelines and platform safe areas.

### Language, evidence, and handoff

- [ ] **S16 | Simple, natural, professional language | 2 points.**
  Are sentences short, active, and easy to say? Use basic words and introduce
  technical terms when the viewer can immediately use them. No word salads,
  corporate copy, forced lowercase, fake intimacy, em dashes, or canned
  cadence such as “It's not X, it's Y.” Do not use “say,” “imagine,” or
  “picture this” as artificial conversational setup. Avoid spoken machine
  specifications unless they are the point.

- [ ] **S17 | One canonical recording script | 1 point.**
  Is the descriptive, keyword-relevant MP4 filename at the top, with one
  `>`-marked narration and clearly separate scene directions? Scripts stay
  in this content repo. Camera viewports belong in the Short's `scenes/`
  directory as `scene_1.cpp`, `scene_2.cpp`, and so on in the code workspace;
  keep the runnable experiment separate. Do not confuse viewport fragments
  with complete compilable programs.

- [ ] **S18 | Relevant evidence, novelty, and useful critique | 2 points.**
  Have we checked the idea ledger, recent Shorts, prior deferrals, and the
  relevant existing Short-form evidence? Identify the evidence and the
  concrete lesson applied. Do not mix long-form retention with Shorts, merge
  platform metrics, infer causation from co-occurrence, or treat comment
  activity as a forecast of demand. Avoid repeating the same lesson under
  another API name without a meaningful new angle. Record useful repairs,
  not cosmetic changes to inflate an iteration count.

- [ ] **S19 | SEO and public-copy path | 1 point.**
  Does the draft identify an accurate primary subject phrase and supporting
  search language without sacrificing the hook to keyword stuffing? Follow
  the separate copy playbook for every public filename, title, caption,
  description, comment, metadata tag, and hashtag set. Research selected
  platforms independently and record unavailable evidence. Candidate terms
  are not SEO approval. No `#shorts`; YouTube descriptions normally have no
  hashtags and use plain text. Do not invent the public copy when it is
  outside the requested scope.

- [ ] **S20 | Honest production and release handoff | 1 point.**
  Are remaining proof, recording, asset, and release checks explicit? Plan
  all four final-export upload checks from the start, but keep unreviewed
  media UNVERIFIED. Include the CapCut audio editing checklist below in the
  production handoff. The publishing package later needs the idea, hook,
  relation to the series, publication window, related YouTube video,
  platform-specific copy, descriptive filename, cover assets, and useful
  first comment where supported. No GitHub link in a YouTube pinned comment.
  Do not manually sort the Shorts playlist without a real sequence.
  Do not publish, schedule, run new analytics, or start an unrequested
  benchmark merely because a script receives a good score.

## CapCut audio editing checklist

For each new Short, Reel, or TikTok edit:

- [ ] Increase the spoken-audio volume by **+5 dB** in CapCut.
- [ ] Enable **Enhance voice** and set **Intensity to 75%**.
- [ ] Check that the settings apply throughout the spoken track, including
  split clips. Apply the gain once, not cumulatively on clips and their group.
- [ ] Listen to the final export for clipping, distortion, and voice-processing
  artifacts. If these settings cause a problem, flag it before upload rather
  than silently change the prescribed settings or approve damaged audio.

Record settings evidence or creator confirmation in `publishing.md`. An exported
audio file alone cannot prove which CapCut settings were used; keep unconfirmed
settings UNVERIFIED. These are creator-directed editing defaults, not measured
causes of reach or a substitute for final-audio review.

## Required sequence summary

`hook -> measured result -> problem -> why is this a problem? -> solution -> why does this solution work? -> next test -> cut`

This is the required order for the whole Short, not only its ending. The first
question asks the audience to diagnose the problem before the answer appears.
The second asks them to explain the revealed solution. Do not silently merge or
substitute them. Keep both compact, preserve the completed lesson, and cut
earlier repetition to fit them. They invite useful participation; they are not
generic comment calls to action.

## Scoring and decision

Use the weights above, totaling **100 points**:

| Status | Credit | Meaning |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| PASS | Full item weight | The draft explicitly and convincingly satisfies the script-level check. Cite where. |
| PARTIAL | Half item weight | A real attempt exists but has a named weakness or missing component. |
| FAIL | Zero | The item is absent, contradicted, or inadequately handled. |
| UNVERIFIED | Zero | Required evidence or the relevant part of the draft is unavailable. Name what is needed. |

Sum awarded points; retain half-points rather than rounding a weak item up.
Do not drop items from the denominator or silently mark them N/A. For limited
fragments, label the total a **provisional partial-draft score**, not the score
of a complete Short.

The score assesses the written script and production plan. A concrete visual
plan can pass its script-level design check without proving rendered quality.
Actual duration, first-frame rendering, annotations, movement, crops, and audio
still need their own final-media review. Benchmark timing verification belongs
to the benchmark/production owner and the pre-upload technical gate, not the
script writer or script score. Missing measurements must stay clearly marked,
but are not a script failure or hold. Script approval does not verify results
or authorize publishing placeholders as facts.

S01, S02, S03, S05, S06, S07, S08, S09, S10, and S14 are mandatory for
advancement. A weak hook, misleading proof, early solution reveal, missing
audience question, broken narrative order, or overlong draft cannot be averaged
away by good formatting.

- **Advance:** all mandatory items pass, and no other FAIL or UNVERIFIED item
  remains within the script task. List any partial items and final-media work.
  This is an editorial recommendation, not creator approval or upload approval.
- **Rework:** fixable wording, structure, visual-plan, or pacing problems remain.
- **Hold:** an unresolved correctness problem in the explanation, contradiction
  with supplied facts, or an unclear premise prevents an honest script-level
  recommendation. Pending benchmark measurements alone do not trigger a hold.

Finish with `Score: X/100. Decision: advance/rework/hold.`
State the biggest remaining weakness in the critique before that line.
Do not call the score a predicted retention, engagement, or reach result, and
do not hand out full credit because the writer intended to satisfy a rule.
