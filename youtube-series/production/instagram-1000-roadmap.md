# Instagram Roadmap to 1,000 Followers

## Baseline

Snapshot: August 22, 2026.

- Followers: 15
- Published Reels: 3
- Summed Reel reach: 13,049, with possible audience overlap
- Short 1: 1,051 reach, 7.89 seconds average watch time, 9 interactions
- Short 2: 10,594 reach, 16.16 seconds average watch time, 118 interactions
- Short 3: 1,404 reach, 15.61 seconds average watch time, 31 interactions

The rough account-level conversion is 15 followers from 13,049 summed reach,
or about 0.115%. This is not a per-Reel attribution because Instagram does not
currently provide us with the source of every follow and audiences may overlap.

At the current rough rate, gaining another 985 followers would require about
857,000 additional reached accounts. The plan must improve both reach and the
percentage of reached viewers who choose to follow.

## Time horizon

- Stretch target: 1,000 followers in 90 days
- Realistic working target: 1,000 followers in 4-8 months
- First decision window: after 12 additional Reels, not after each post

The 90-day target requires approximately 25 new followers per Reel at three
Reels per week. At a 0.25% follow conversion rate, that requires about 10,000
reach per Reel on average. Short 2 proves that this scale is possible once, not
that it is already repeatable.

## Weekly operating cadence

Publish three original Reels per week:

1. A concrete C++ failure, contradiction, or surprising runtime result.
2. A machine-learning idea explained through one visible numerical problem.
3. A systems or performance experiment with a measured result.

Keep each Reel native, self-contained, and approximately 18-25 seconds when the
idea permits it. Put the first result or contradiction inside the first three
seconds. Use progressive visual disclosure and no CTA, or a CTA shorter than two
seconds.

Support the Reels with:

- one technical carousel per week when the topic benefits from a diagram or
  compact derivation;
- two to four informal Stories per week showing production, questions, polls,
  corrections, or follow-up results;
- timely replies to substantive comments, especially questions that can become
  future Reel ideas.

Long-form episode production remains the source of ideas and credibility, but
Instagram Reels must stand alone. Do not make understanding a Reel depend on a
YouTube episode.

## First 12-Reel experiment

Keep the production language stable enough to compare topics:

- direct spoken hook;
- one concept;
- one visual contradiction or observable result;
- camera plus the established visual system;
- no introductory greeting;
- no external watermark;
- safe-area-compliant cover and composition.

Test these three topic families four times each:

- C++ failures and language traps;
- ML mathematics made concrete;
- performance and hardware behavior.

Change one major variable at a time. Do not simultaneously change the hook,
runtime, visual style, topic, and CTA, because the result will not tell us what
caused the difference.

## Profile conversion

Reach is useful only if the profile makes the continuing value obvious.

- Keep the name field searchable: `Kerem Kazan | C++ & Deep Learning`.
- Make the bio promise concise: deep learning, modern C++, and systems from
  first principles.
- Keep the complete YouTube playlist as the primary link.
- Pin three posts that together explain the account: the strongest C++ Reel,
  the strongest ML Reel, and a short series-orientation post.
- Keep Reel covers visually consistent so the profile reads as one body of
  work rather than unrelated clips.
- Test a sub-two-second follow line on only one of every three Reels. Compare
  follow conversion without assuming the CTA helps.

## Measurement

Capture each Reel at 24 hours, 72 hours, and seven days:

- reach;
- plays;
- average watch time and average percentage viewed;
- likes, comments, saves, shares, and total interactions;
- follower count before publication and at each checkpoint;
- profile visits and Reel-attributed follows when Instagram exposes them.

Use these account-specific experimental thresholds for the next 12 Reels:

- average percentage viewed: at least 65%;
- interactions divided by reach: at least 1.5%;
- saves plus shares divided by reach: at least 0.5%;
- follow conversion: work toward at least 0.25%;
- distribution: raise median reach above 5,000 and produce at least three Reels
  above 10,000 reach.

These are operating targets derived from the current account, not universal
Instagram benchmarks.

## Milestones

### 15 to 100 followers

- Publish the first 12-Reel experiment.
- Establish which two topic families repeatedly earn reach and watch time.
- Improve the profile and pinned-post path.
- Do not chase collaborations or paid promotion yet.

### 100 to 300 followers

- Turn winning topics into named recurring formats.
- Publish sequels quickly when a Reel earns meaningful saves, shares, or
  questions.
- Test relevant collaboration posts with people or communities where a real
  relationship already exists, including AI NYC when appropriate.
- Add one thoughtful carousel per week to deepen profile value.

### 300 to 1,000 followers

- Concentrate most Reels in the two proven formats while reserving roughly 20%
  for new experiments.
- Increase to four Reels per week only if quality and long-form production stay
  intact.
- Use comment questions and prior winners to build connected sequences rather
  than isolated posts.
- Consider selective collaborations, guest explanations, and response videos.

## Review rules

- Review tactics after every six Reels.
- Review the overall Instagram strategy after every twelve Reels.
- Do not change direction because one Reel stalls or one Reel breaks out.
- Compare median performance, not only the largest winner.
- Separate reach, retention, interaction, profile conversion, and follower
  growth when diagnosing a result.
