1. **Does the opening still hide a prerequisite?** The helper uses `TensorNode`
   before its definition. The script now labels this incomplete integration,
   explains the result/input/gradient references as they appear, and defines the
   type next. It must not be presented as an already executable backward pass.
   This is a permanent local rule, not an intentional compiler-error demo.
2. **Is one minute credible?** The opening has one short promise, the sum rule,
   and five lines of code. About 0:45-1:00 is an editorial estimate. Rehearse it.
   The completed engine still comes later; do not advertise an early terminal
   result or count storage initialization as propagated gradients.
3. **Is the main integration stretch still dense?** Yes. A-B contain ownership,
   the mechanical storage replacement, and several result-history edits. L11
   stays PARTIAL. Reuse known code visibly and cut typing pauses, not the new
   ownership contract or the reason for operation tags.
4. **Does backward silently stop at matrix multiplication?** No. History includes
   matmul, and final backward rejects a reached matmul before resetting any
   gradient. C and D also reject operations not supported at their stage.
5. **Do the same gradients come from real code?** The revised snippets compile
   in temporary copies of the actual source. Sum gives `1 1`; the scalar offset
   collects `2`; MSE gives loss `1` and prediction gradients `1 -1`. Numerical
   tests cover broadcast and shared-input cases. See [verification.md](verification.md).
6. **Is the scope complete and accurately packaged?** Prediction gradients
   fulfill the recorded continuation. Matrix backward remains the next capability.
   Candidate subject terms and filename are accurate; platform keyword research
   is pending, so L15 remains PARTIAL. No release copy is approved.
