# Audience growth checkpoint: August 23, 2026

## Verdict

The series is on track for its current stage: it has produced measurable
audience growth and at least one meaningful discovery event during its first
eleven days. It is not yet producing repeatable or compounding growth.

This is a positive early checkpoint, not proof of a durable growth engine.

## What produced the audience

### YouTube

The channel has gained nine subscribers since the first complete baseline.
The available per-video Analytics rows attribute eight subscribers to Episode
1, three to Episode 3, one to Episode 4, and one to Short 2. Those are gross
attributions across differently processed windows, so they should not be added
and treated as net channel growth.

The important signal is qualitative: long-form videos with weak average
consumption have still converted a small number of viewers into subscribers.
The subject and series premise appear attractive to the right viewer even
though opening retention remains the larger long-form constraint.

Public long-form views currently form an age-dependent staircase rather than a
rising view floor: Episode 1 has 231, Episode 2 has 93, Episode 3 has 62,
Episode 4 has 42, and Episode 5 has 41. Episode 5 reaching Episode 4's current
public count after only two days is mildly encouraging, but processed watch
data is not yet available and the sample is too small for a trend claim.

### Instagram

The three Reels currently report:

| Short | Plays | Accounts reached | Average watch | Interactions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Short 1 | 1,195 | 1,059 | 7.94 seconds | 9 |
| Short 2 | 15,075 | 10,644 | 16.17 seconds | 118 |
| Short 3 | 2,966 | 2,101 | 15.42 seconds | 47 |

Short 2 proves that Instagram can place this material in front of a cold
audience. Short 3 has not repeated Short 2's reach, but its watch time remains
close and its interaction rate per play is currently higher. This suggests the
creative is not failing after it starts; distribution and follower conversion
remain separate questions.

The three reach figures sum to 13,804, although the same account may appear in
more than one Reel's reach. Seventeen followers against that non-deduplicated
reach is roughly a 0.12% account-level conversion proxy. It is not an exact
per-Reel conversion rate, but it shows why another 1,000 followers will require
either much more reach, better conversion, or both.

### X and LinkedIn

Follower movement is small. These platforms are currently serving as
credibility, relationships, and distribution support rather than meaningful
audience-growth engines. That is acceptable during the experiment, but their
announcement work should remain bounded so it does not displace production.

## Are we on track?

### Yes, for the current thirty-day experiment

- The audience is larger on every measured platform.
- Instagram has produced one five-figure discovery event.
- YouTube has grown by more than half from the first tracked baseline.
- Multiple long-form episodes have converted viewers into subscribers.
- Shorts 2 and 3 both earned materially larger initial tests than Short 1.

### Not yet, for dependable compounding growth

- Only one Short has crossed 10,000 plays.
- The long-form view floor has not clearly increased yet.
- Instagram follower conversion is still small relative to reach.
- X and LinkedIn follower counts are nearly flat.
- TikTok has no usable analytics baseline.
- Returning-viewer growth and movement from Shorts into long-form remain
  unproven.

At the current linear pace, neither YouTube nor Instagram reaches 1,000 soon.
That extrapolation is not the actual strategy. The strategy depends on
improving creative quality, repeating discovery, and allowing a library of
content to compound.

## What to do next

1. Protect Episode 6 and the regular production cadence. Do not redirect time
   into more distribution channels because one Reel performed well.
2. Publish Shorts 4 through 6 using the stable, front-loaded structure from the
   current experiment. Change one meaningful variable at a time.
3. Treat follower conversion as an explicit experiment. Test one concise,
   sub-two-second series promise at the end of one Short while leaving the
   others without a CTA.
4. Preserve the long-form opening experiment: expose the concrete problem
   immediately, begin meaningful implementation within the first few minutes,
   and create observable intermediate results.
5. Reassess after Short 6 and Episode 8. The important question is whether
   discovery and subscriber conversion repeat, not whether one post continues
   accumulating views.
6. Make the first broader strategic decision around day thirty, after Short 9.

## Next decision test

The next three Shorts should answer:

- Can at least two ideas earn meaningful cold distribution rather than relying
  on one breakout?
- Can the series turn that reach into followers more efficiently?
- Do those followers watch another Short or enter the long-form series?

Until those results exist, maintain the positioning and cadence.
