## Diagnosis

Short 5 is under-distributed, but the current evidence does not show that it
failed because viewers rejected it. It received almost no Shorts-feed test.
The upload is technically eligible and no metadata, format, audience, policy,
or processing defect is visible.

The most plausible explanations are:

1. YouTube had low confidence about which cold audience should receive the
   Short. The channel is still small, its recent audience includes people who
   arrived through a deep-learning and autograd Reddit post, and this Short is
   about CPU instruction dependencies. That mismatch may make initial audience
   selection harder.
2. Normal recommendation variance and competition prevented or delayed a seed
   test. YouTube does not guarantee equal feed exposure for consecutive Shorts,
   even when their upload settings match.
3. The first frame and opening may be less immediately legible than Short 4,
   but the available feed sample is far too small to support this as the cause.
   The opening shows a small code block and `Inefficient loop?`; the complete
   one-variable promise arrives through speech and captions over several beats.

Short 5 published at 7:15 PM ET. Record that time as an experimental variable,
not a diagnosed cause. Short 4 also published on a Tuesday evening at 7:27 PM
ET and received a normal Shorts-feed test, so the current channel evidence does
not isolate evening publication as the difference. YouTube also states that
publication time is not known to affect long-term video performance, although
publishing while an audience is active can help early viewership. Test an
earlier Short slot while holding the topic and opening structure as constant as
practical before changing the standing cadence.

The following are not supported causes:

- the 7:15 PM publication time as a proven cause, because it closely matches
  Short 4's successful Tuesday evening release;
- missing automatic captions;
- the missing related-video assignment;
- custom-thumbnail behavior inside the Shorts feed; or
- a demonstrated bad stayed-versus-swiped rate.
