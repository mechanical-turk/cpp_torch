# Thirty-day content experiment plan: August 22, 2026

## Current diagnosis

Nine days is enough to evaluate individual pieces, but not enough to decide
whether the channel model works. The series already has early traction:

- YouTube grew from the first tracked baseline of 16 subscribers on August 17
  to 24 subscribers on August 22.
- Short 2 reached roughly 15,000 Instagram plays and more than 10,000 accounts.
- Short 3 received YouTube's largest initial test so far, reaching roughly
  1,300 Studio views in its first three hours, and its Instagram version passed
  1,700 plays during its opening afternoon.
- Episode 4's small processed sample improved to 82 seconds average duration
  and 10.38% average viewed, compared with Episode 3's 24 seconds and 1.19%.

These results show that individual ideas can earn distribution and that the
long-form restructuring may be helping. They do not yet show repeatability or
a returning audience.

## Review schedule

Continue for six more Shorts, organized as two rounds of three, over roughly
three weeks.

### Round 1: Shorts 4 through 6

Repeat the strongest current structure:

- one concrete C++ failure, contradiction, or surprising result;
- the explanatory result inside the first three to five seconds;
- one causal mechanism;
- approximately 20 to 25 seconds when the subject permits it; and
- native publication on YouTube, Instagram, X, and TikTok.

Keep the format reasonably stable. Use this round to learn whether the success
of the unsigned-loop and overflow subjects can be repeated.

Review after Short 6. Adjust openings and delivery, but do not change the
series positioning from only three additional observations.

### Round 2: Shorts 7 through 9

Carry forward the best-performing structure from Round 1. Change one major
variable at a time, such as the technical subject, opening visual, or density
of the explanation.

Review after Short 9, which should fall near the series' 30-day mark. This is
the first appropriate point for a broader decision about the Short format,
topic selection, and publishing cadence.

## Long-form experiment

Evaluate Episodes 6 through 8 as one group. They must continue the newer lesson
order:

- the concrete problem appears immediately;
- the opening teaches something instead of advertising a later payoff;
- meaningful code begins within the first few minutes; and
- the episode produces observable intermediate results throughout.

Compare their opening retention, average duration, average percentage viewed,
traffic sources, subscriber conversion, and movement into another episode.
Do not judge the long-form model from Episode 5 until its watch data processes.

## Metrics that decide the next move

For Shorts, compare five separate stages:

1. distribution: shown in feed or accounts reached;
2. opening conversion: stayed to watch versus swiped away;
3. consumption: average duration, percentage watched, completion, and replay;
4. response: likes, comments, saves, and shares; and
5. audience conversion: subscribers, followers, and visits to long-form work.

For long-form, prioritize:

1. retention through the first minute;
2. average duration and percentage viewed;
3. browse, suggested, search, and playlist traffic;
4. subscriber conversion; and
5. returning viewers and viewers who continue into another episode.

The current internal baselines are comparisons, not universal algorithmic
thresholds. Short 2 produced approximately 73% average viewed on YouTube;
Short 3 produced roughly 40% stayed to watch during its first feed test.
Future Shorts should improve one stage without assuming that a single metric
determines distribution.

## Decision gates

- After Short 6 and Episode 8: tactical review. Improve hooks, pacing, and
  topic selection.
- Around day 30, after approximately nine Shorts: decide whether the current
  Short structure is repeatable and whether it brings anyone into the broader
  series.
- Around weeks six to eight: if Shorts still produce only isolated spikes,
  long-form has no improving view floor, and returning-viewer activity remains
  absent, reconsider the content mix and publication cadence.
- Around day 90: if the series has not produced repeatable discovery,
  meaningful returning viewership, or steady subscriber growth, reconsider the
  positioning and format more substantially.

## The play

Shorts introduce one sharp technical idea to strangers. Long-form gives the
right viewers a reason to stay and follow the larger project. Native technical
and personal posts keep the creator recognizable between releases. The
repository makes the work tangible.

The immediate objective is not one viral Short. It is proving that the same
creative principles can repeatedly attract viewers and that some of those
viewers continue into the series.
