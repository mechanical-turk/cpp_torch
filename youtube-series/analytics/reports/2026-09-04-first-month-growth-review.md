## What changed the trajectory

### Instagram found a repeatable discovery format

Short 5 reached 156,478 plays, 90,998 accounts, and 1,775 interactions. It
received 983 likes, 111 comments, 457 saves, and 216 shares. Average watch time
was 18.35 seconds on a 19.77-second master, though loops can raise that metric.

This is 6.3 times the plays of Short 4 and nearly ten times the plays of Short
2. It accounts for 77.9% of the 200,994 Instagram plays across the four indexed
Reels. Instagram followers rose from 42 before publication to 154, an
account-level gain of 112. The Reel had already attributed 74 follows when the
learner captured its engagement screen, so the breakout clearly converted some
viewers while not proving that every later follow came from the Reel.

The creative combined several useful signals: an immediate claim, a visible
code target, the `Inefficient loop?` text hook, emphasized captions, a measured
result, and a real technical question. The end-of-Reel question encouraged
discussion. The next-experiment framing opened a path to future content without
asking viewers to follow.

The opening still has room to improve. The retention curve lost viewers
quickly in the first three seconds and ended at 43%. The format succeeded in
spite of that early loss, not because the opening was finished.

### Reddit became the strongest qualified external channel

The first r/learnmachinelearning milestone post reached 21,000 views and 115
upvotes within 48 hours and became the top post of the day. YouTube moved from
46 subscribers immediately before the post to 59 at the 48-hour checkpoint,
while Episode 7 gained 323 public views. The timing strongly links Reddit to
the jump, but delayed attribution prevents assigning every view or subscriber
to Reddit.

The post worked because it showed a concrete technical milestone, included a
useful visual, explained the implementation, and asked a design question the
community could answer. Reddit should remain a weekly research and distribution
channel, but only when there is a community-specific milestone or discussion
worth posting.

### YouTube long form converts subscribers better than Shorts

The processed long-form rows attribute 32 net subscribers to Episodes 1-7.
The processed Shorts rows attribute one subscriber. Reporting lag and external
traffic leave part of the channel's total gain unattributed, but the split is
large enough to guide the strategy: Shorts create reach, while the series and
external technical communities currently create most YouTube subscribers.

Episode 7 reached 744 public views, ahead of Episode 1 at 465 and Episode 5 at
352. It also recorded five subscribers gained and one lost in the processed
Analytics row. The result combines several simultaneous changes: a stronger
topic, a face-led thumbnail, the Reddit post, and a shorter opening. It does
not isolate any one of them.

Long-form retention remains the main production problem. Episode 7 averaged
112 seconds and 7.95% viewed. Its early retention falls sharply by roughly the
first 30 seconds. Cold intros are directionally correct, but the opening must
begin the actual problem and useful work even faster.

### YouTube did not give Short 5 a meaningful cold-feed test

Short 5 has 152 public views on YouTube. The earlier Studio audit found only
25% from the Shorts feed and 54.3% from Search, with strong replay among the
small group who stayed. The upload is public, unrestricted, vertical 4K, and
configured like the other Shorts. This supports an audience-matching or seed-
distribution miss more than a creative-rejection diagnosis. Do not reupload
the unchanged video.

### X and LinkedIn are not growth engines yet

X moved from 224 to 230 followers and LinkedIn from 755 to 760. Keep them as
professional distribution and relationship channels, but do not spend the
next production cycle optimizing them at the expense of Instagram, YouTube,
or Reddit.

## Priorities for the next four weeks

1. Produce another high-quality Short using the Short 5 structure. Improve only
   the first three seconds: one simple spoken promise, a strong visual target,
   a short text hook, emphasized caption words, and immediate motion or proof.
2. Preserve the discussion question at the end. Pair it with a specific next
   experiment so the Reel earns comments and creates a reason to return.
3. Turn the next three Shorts into a recognizable C++ performance sequence.
   Keep each lesson self-contained, but make the next experiment visible.
4. Keep long-form cold opens. Show the bug, result, or code problem immediately;
   start meaningful coding within 30 seconds and deliver the first payoff within
   one minute. Remove setup, repeated context, and low-value tests.
5. Use Reddit for milestones and design discussions, not episode spam. Prepare
   the next post around a real tensor-autograd integration milestone and take
   24-hour, 48-hour, 72-hour, and seven-day snapshots.
6. Measure the funnel by platform. For Instagram, record reach, three-second
   loss, completion, comments, shares, saves, profile visits, and follows. For
   YouTube Shorts, record shown-in-feed, stayed-to-watch, watch time, and
   subscribers. Do not merge those definitions.

## Bottom line

This is not a failing first month. Two independent discovery events have
already happened: Reddit delivered qualified long-form traffic, and Instagram
distributed a Reel to more than 90,000 accounts. YouTube subscribers grew
4.4 times from the first baseline. The problem is no longer whether the work
can earn attention. The next challenge is repetition: reproduce the Short 5
format without copying the topic, keep Reddit useful to its communities, and
turn the larger reach into a clearer reason to watch the series and return for
the next experiment.
